# Project licence and provider terms

Corrected 16 October 2026.

## Corrected terms review

- Generated application code and generated project artwork are Output under
  [Grok Consumer Terms section 4](https://x.ai/legal/terms-of-service).
  The [official FAQ](https://x.ai/legal/faq) permits using outputs, including
  commercially. Do not classify all exported source as proprietary platform code.
- Pre-existing platform implementations and third-party assets retain their own
  terms (Consumer Terms sections 3 and 6). The inventory alone is neither a
  publication ban nor a legal clearance. No clause was found forbidding this
  project's publication merely because the listed files arrived in an export.
- Preserve accurate Grok attribution and unaltered marks under the
  [Brand Guidelines](https://x.ai/legal/brand-guidelines). Dependency licences and
  notices remain intact. Original Out2Eat code is MIT under Benedict Okpala;
  that grant does not relicense other parties' assets or Qloo data.
- The [2026 Qloo event requirements](https://qloo.devpost.com/) require a public,
  externally hosted demo and public open-source repository. Official Rules
  section 4 requires judging access through the judging period.
- The [hackathon developer guide](https://docs.qloo.com/reference/qloo-llm-hackathon-developer-guide#can-i-cache-qloo-api-responses-in-my-app)
  expressly permits private server-side caches without a retention time limit
  and forbids Qloo response data in public repositories. Search evidence is
  cached privately for 300 seconds; successful tags 86400 seconds; misses 1800.
  These implemented durations do not require separate retention permission.
- Hackathon keys remain active through judging and are deactivated after the
  hackathon closes (guide: Quotas, Limits & Key Expiration). Public judging demos
  are in scope; ongoing post-hackathon/commercial service needs renewed access.

Earlier blanket licence/hosting/cache blockers are withdrawn. No additional
agreement or export-specific prohibition was supplied or found. Any narrower
file-origin question is a provenance question, not an automatic deployment veto.
